How a river bend moves

A river is never one speed across. Around a bend the water on the outside runs faster and the water on the inside slower. The fast side scours its bank; the slow side can't hold its sand and drops it. One bank retreats, the other builds out, and the whole bend walks sideways, and a little downstream, across its valley.

What you are looking at

The tank holds a model valley 850 m long and 400 m across, with its heights stretched five times so the banks read. The cut bank is the cliff on the outside of a bend: the water undercuts it and it slumps in, boulders and all. The point bar is the sandy slope on the inside, built from the sand the slow water drops. The ridges on it, the scroll bars, each mark where the inner bank stood a few years earlier; grass, then shrubs, then trees move onto them as they age. When a loop grows until its neck is barely wider than the river, the river breaks through: the loop is left behind as an oxbow lake that slowly fills in.

The current you can't see

In a bend the surface water is flung outward and the water near the bed is swept inward: a slow corkscrew called the helical (or secondary) flow. The glowing cross-section shows it, cyan where it runs outward at the surface, amber where it carries sand back along the bed. It is why the sand ends up on the inside. Tap the river, or the map on the console, to move the section.

The controls

Flow sets the discharge, 20 to 400 m³/s. The bank holds until the water beside it runs faster than 1.35 m/s, so at low flow nothing erodes; the red mark on the flow slider is the flow at which the sharpest bend first gives way. Sediment is how much sand the river carries: with more, the bars build faster and push the bend along; with none, the bars can't keep up and the channel widens instead. Years runs the clock at 2, 8 or 30 years a second; turn the dial on the console, or drag the slider, to go back or forward. On the tank: the inflow valve steps the flow, the hopper steps the sediment.

What is exact and what is not

The migration is the model river scientists use (Ikeda, Parker and Sawai, in Howard and Knutson's form): the speed by each bank comes from how sharply the channel curves there and just upstream; the bank erodes by how far its shear stress exceeds a threshold; sand settles where the water is slow; loops that touch cut off. The numbers are realistic for a river about 70 m wide. The heights are stretched 5×, the water and sand are shown about 40× faster than real so you can follow them, and the tank's inlet swings slowly to keep new bends coming, as meander flumes do.
