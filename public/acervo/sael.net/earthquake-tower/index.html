Why a building hates one rhythm

Push a child on a swing once per swing and it goes higher every time; push at a random beat and it barely moves. A building is a swing standing on its end. Its floors are heavy, its columns are springy, so it has a rhythm of its own: nudge it and it rocks back and forth at that beat. Here two model towers stand on a shake table, a platform that slides back and forth at whatever beat you choose.

What you see

When the table's beat matches a tower's own rhythm, every push lands in step and the sway builds until the roof swings about twelve times as far as the table moves. Slower than that, the tower just rides along like a stiff block. Faster, the ground slides back and forth under a roof that hardly moves. The glowing curve on the middle panel is that story for every beat at once: each tower has a peak at its own rhythm, and the marker is where the table is now. The side panels draw each tower's own rhythm next to the table's.

What changes the rhythm

Height: more floors means more mass on springs of the same stiffness, so a slower rhythm. Engineers' rule of thumb is about 0.1 seconds of sway per storey, which is exactly what these towers do when unbraced. Braces: a steel X in a storey makes that storey three times stiffer and the rhythm faster. Where you put them matters: bracing the bottom storeys moves the rhythm far more than bracing the top ones, and leaving one storey unbraced under braced ones makes it a soft storey that takes most of the bending.

What you can do

Rhythm: drag the slider or turn the dial on the middle panel, tap the chevrons, tap a side panel to shake at that tower's rhythm, or use the card. Braces: click any storey on a tower. Height: drag a tower's roof up or down. Hold peak (the button on the panel, the card, or Space) freezes the next biggest sway and measures both towers. Quake shakes at many rhythms at once; each tower picks out its own. Click an actuator to kick the table once and watch each tower ring at its own beat.

What is exact and what is not

Each tower is a shear building: one mass per floor, one spring per storey, 5 % damping, solved exactly 600 times a second. Its rhythms come from an eigen-analysis of those masses and springs, and the curve from the same modes. The numbers are for full-size buildings (3 m storeys for A, 3.8 m for B) and a table that keeps its peak speed at 25 cm/s, like the middle of a strong quake's spectrum. All motion is drawn 12 times larger than life so you can see it. Real buildings crack, yield and soften, which caps the peak; this model stays elastic.
