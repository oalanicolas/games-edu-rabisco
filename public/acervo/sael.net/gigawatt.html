How to play

You start with one gaming PC in a garage. Every request it answers earns money. Spend it, grow the datacenter, and train better models until you reach AGI.

Three meters, top left

  • Served is how much of what your users ask for you answer. The rest get 429 Too Many Requests, and you lose that money. Build racks.
  • Cooled is how many racks have enough cooling. Every watt a rack uses comes out as heat, and a rack without enough cooling throttles and glows red. A cooler takes heat from the eight tiles around it, up to its rating. Bigger racks need stronger cooling: a box fan can cool a PC, a server needs an air handler, a 72-GPU rack needs liquid.
  • Power is what you draw. Over what you can supply, everything slows down. Buy more in Power.

When a meter turns red, click it, or the button in the box under it: the game picks the right thing to build, and the glowing tile shows where. When the floor fills up, expand it.

Serve, then train

Your compute answers users first. What they do not use trains your next model (and a tenth is always kept for training). When training finishes, launch: a better model brings far more users, a funding round, and new hardware to build.

What waits for you

  • Building takes time. What you place goes up behind scaffolding, a few things at once: one for every building crew. Click a site to see how long it has left, or to finish it now for money.
  • Datacenter hardware comes by truck. From the first server on, what you order waits at the curb for your signature: click the truck, or press Enter. A driver waits a minute, then takes it back for a later truck and a small fee. Nothing is lost.
  • Bill your users. What they owe waits at the fiber hut, about a minute of it. Click the bubble or the amount next to your money, or press Enter. When it is full, what they use goes unbilled.
  • Racks go down now and then: their lights flash red and they serve nothing until you bring them back. Click one, the bubble over it, or press Enter.
  • Away, your lab works one night shift, then waits for you. In Staff, an assistant does the chores while you play, and your own models take them over as they get smarter.

Controls

Pick something in Build (or press 1 to 9), then click a tile, or drag across the floor to fill a row. Click anything you built to upgrade or sell it. Enter bills your users, signs for every delivery and brings back every rack that is down. Esc puts the tool down. Drag elsewhere to orbit, right-drag to pan, scroll to zoom, WASD to move, Q E to turn, C for the cinematic cameras, R to reset the view, M for sound, / to hide the interface.

Graphics

The sliders button next to sound sets the graphics level. Auto picks one for your device and steps down if the game stutters. On an older laptop or a phone, Low keeps it smooth.

What is real

The hardware is modelled on real machines: about 10 kW for an 8-GPU server, about 130 kW for a 72-GPU liquid-cooled rack, about 600 kW for the 576-GPU racks announced for 2027. The research upgrades are real techniques. Money and time are sped up so a chapter takes minutes instead of years, and the last tier of hardware is speculative.
