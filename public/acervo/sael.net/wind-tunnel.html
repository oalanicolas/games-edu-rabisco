Why tucking in is faster

Ride at 40 km/h on the flat and about four fifths of your effort goes into shoving air out of the way. That push back is drag: half the air's density, times your speed squared, times your drag area (CdA), which is the size of the hole you punch in the air weighted by how cleanly the air closes behind you.

The ribbons

Each glowing line is a thread of smoke carried by the air, drawn at an eighth of its real speed. The bright pulses travel with it, so where they bunch up the air is slowing down. Amber is air piling up against the rider: high pressure, pushing back. Bright blue is air speeding round the shoulders and helmet. Where it cannot follow the body round, it tears away into a churning wake, a pocket of low pressure that pulls back. The size of the wake is the drag.

Position

Sitting up, the chest is a wall facing the wind. On the hoods it tilts. Tucked, with a flat back and the head low, the air slides over the back and the wake shrinks; the legs, which cannot hide because they are pedalling, become the biggest part of the drag.

Frame

An aero frame has tall, thin tubes with a cut-off airfoil tail (a Kamm tail) and deep carbon rims, so the air leaves them cleanly instead of spinning off round tubes. It saves about 20 W at 40 km/h; the red semi-aero bike, with deeper tubes and 45 mm rims, about half that. The rider is still most of the drag: about four fifths of it on any bike. Pick a bike on the card, press ⇧F, click the bike in the tunnel or one on the plinths: a ring of light runs along it with the wind and leaves the new bike behind it.

The smoke wand

Press ⇧T for the operator's smoke wand, then point into the tunnel to move it (a black backdrop rolls down behind the glass so the smoke shows, as in a real tunnel). Its smoke is carried by the same air as the ribbons: point at the helmet and watch it split, run over the back and break up in the wake.

Ride the air

Press ⇧V to ride one line of air from the honeycomb, over the rider's helmet and down his back into the wake, to the fan. Click or scroll to get off. M turns the sound on: the wind grows with the speed and gets rougher the bigger the wake.

Wind

Drag grows with the square of the speed, and the power to beat it with the cube: going twice as fast takes eight times the power. The wake keeps its shape as the wind rises (that is why a single CdA works at every speed), but it pulls harder and churns faster.

Pressure

Press ⇧P to paint the air's pressure on the rider. Red faces take the wind head on and are pushed; blue and violet faces are sucked at by fast air and by the wake. Drag is the front pushing and the back pulling, added together.

The tunnel

Air comes in through the honeycomb on the right, which straightens it, passes the rider on a trainer that stands on a balance (that is what measures the drag) and leaves through the fan on the left, which pulls it through, as in most small tunnels.
