How the heart pumps

Your heart is two pumps side by side. The right side (blue here) takes blood that has come back from the body and pushes it through the lungs. The left side (red) takes the blood that comes back from the lungs and pushes it out to the body. One squeeze drives both loops at once: that is the figure of eight, and it is why the two return pipes cross to the other side of the heart.

One beat

Between beats the valves into the ventricles hang open and blood pours in (filling); the atria give a last squeeze. Then the ventricles contract: the inflow valves slam shut (the lub) and for a moment all four valves are closed while the pressure climbs. When it beats the pressure in the arteries, the outflow valves open and blood rushes out (ejection). As the ventricles relax the outflow valves snap shut (the dub) and the cycle starts again. The console shows the pressures and the ventricle's volume through one beat; drag its steel thumb, the card's timeline, or press [ ] to step through it.

Valves keep it one way

A valve only opens when the pressure behind it is higher than the pressure ahead. Click a valve (or 1–4) to jam it: open, and blood runs back through it on every beat (the coral arrows); narrow, and the heart has to push much harder to get less through. Watch the pressures, the output and the heart's size change. 0 fixes them all.

What is real

The flows, pressures and volumes come from a lumped model of the circulation stepped 4,000 times a second: four chambers that stiffen and relax on a clock, four valves, arteries that stretch, veins that hold most of the blood. At rest it gives about 70 mL a beat, 5 litres a minute, an aortic pressure near 120/80 and 25/10 in the lungs' artery. Blood is not blue: oxygen-poor blood is dark red, blue is just the textbook code. The cells are drawn a thousand times too big, a lap here takes about thirteen beats (a real one takes about a minute), and the atria's movement is exaggerated so you can see it.
