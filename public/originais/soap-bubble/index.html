How a soap film solves geometry

A soap film is two layers of soap molecules with a little water between them. Its surface tension pulls it tight like a stretched sheet, so the film keeps shrinking until it can't shrink any more. What's left is the smallest surface that fits the wire: a minimal surface.

What you see

The page does what the film does. The film is a mesh of about 1,800 points. Every step, each point moves the way that shrinks the area fastest, while the wire (and the ring, when it has caught the film) holds the edges. When nothing moves any more, the mesh is a minimal surface. The area and the energy (two faces times the surface tension, 25 mN/m for soapy water) fall while it settles.

A flat wire gives a flat film. Twist the wire and the film curves into a saddle. At 90° the square's four wires are four edges of a regular tetrahedron, the surface H. A. Schwarz worked out in 1865. A triangle can only turn, never twist: any three corners lie in one plane, so its film stays flat.

Pull the ring out through the film and the film clings to it as a funnel. Its waist, the neck, gets thinner the further you pull, and the ring's pull gets weaker (it is about 4πγ times the neck's radius). Past a certain point no funnel can exist at all. The neck pinches, and the film snaps into two separate flat films: one on the wire and one on the ring. For two equal rings this happens when the gap is 1.3255 times the radius. Push the ring back into the film and it catches again.

The colours

Light bounces off both faces of the film. The two reflections meet a little out of step, and the step depends on the film's thickness (a few hundred nanometres) and the angle you look from. Some colours cancel and others add up. The page does that sum over the whole visible spectrum. Gravity drains the film downwards, so the bands lie level and thin out towards the top, until the top turns black: a film about 10 nm thick is too thin to reflect anything. Black film is fragile, so that is usually where it bursts.

Hands on

Drag the ring (or its knob) to pull it. Drag the console knob or the wire's bottom bar to twist it, and use the knob's arrows to change the frame. Press the drop button or click the dish for a fresh film. Click the film itself to pop it.
