How to play

You build one humanoid robot by hand, on a workbench in a garage. Every robot you finish walks out to the gate, and there you decide what it does next:

  • Ship it. It is sold, and the money pays for everything: stations, actuator cells, power, floor and research.
  • Keep it. It walks back in and joins the crew of one of your stations, where it builds more robots: kept robots staff your stations first. A kept robot with no station to work at practises in the yard, and that practice trains the next brain.

Set the split with the slider in the card, the lever on the gate, or [ and ]. Ship too many and nothing grows. Keep too many and you cannot pay for anything.

Three meters, top left

  • Orders: how many of the robots customers order you send them. Who wants a robot depends on what it can do, so every new brain brings far more orders. A robot the gate would ship with no truck waiting for it is kept instead.
  • Parts: stations with enough actuators, the motors in every joint. Each robot takes 40. An actuator cell feeds the eight tiles around it, up to its rating, and better stations need better actuators.
  • Power: what your stations, your actuator cells and every robot on charge draw, against what you can supply. Buy more in Power.

When a meter turns red, click it, or the button in the box under it: the game picks the right thing, and the glowing tile shows where.

On the floor

  • The gate. Finished robots wait in line at the gate until someone sends them through: click the bubble over it, or press Enter. When the line is full, the stations stop.
  • The dock. Orders come as trucks. Robots you ship board the first one waiting, and a loaded truck waits for your signature: click it, or press Enter. It pays when it leaves. A driver who waits too long leaves without the robots: they stay yours, but orders dip for a while. Rush orders pay double.
  • Breakdowns. Now and then a station jams: its lights flash red and it builds nothing until someone fixes it. Click it, the bubble over it, or press Enter.
  • Building. Everything you build goes up behind scaffolding and takes days, one thing per crew at a time. Every floor you add brings another crew, and you can hire more. Click a site to see how long it has to go, or to cancel it for what you paid.
  • Staff. Hire an assistant for the gate or the dock, or a technician for repairs: they work while you are here. Once a brain can do the job, robots take over, for free, and keep working when you are away.

Pay to finish a build now: half its price for the time it has left (click the site, or its timer). When you leave, the factory works one night shift, then waits for you.

The first minutes are a tour: it points at the one thing to do next and says why. Skip it any time.

The brain

Robots practising in the yard teach the next brain. When the bar fills, update: every robot you have ever built gets the new brain at once, over the air. Better brains work faster, bring many more orders, and unlock new hardware and a funding round.

The goal

Ten million robots a year. Every robot you build counts toward your nation: you will pass the population of Tuvalu, Monaco, Iceland and further.

Controls

Pick something in Build (or press 1 to 9), then click a tile, or drag across the floor to fill a row. Click anything you built to upgrade or sell it. Click the robot on the workbench to help build it. Enter sends the line at the gate through, signs for loaded trucks and fixes jammed stations. Esc puts the tool down. Drag elsewhere to orbit, right-drag to pan, scroll to zoom, WASD to move, Q E to turn, C for the cinematic cameras, R to reset the view, M for sound, / to hide the interface.

What is real

A humanoid robot has about 28 actuators in its body and more in its hands, a battery of about 2.3 kWh, and charging that averages a few hundred watts. The brains follow how humanoid robots are actually learning, from people driving them in VR headsets to models that learn a new job from one demonstration. Ten million a year is the target announced for the biggest humanoid robot factory being built. Money and time are sped up: a day passes every second, and the last tier of hardware is speculative.
